energy poverty remains an important challenge for sustainable development in china with pronounced regional disparities and evolving temporal dynamics that require accurate and interpretable prediction tools
this study develops a provincial panel-based framework that combines energy poverty index epi construction ssa-lstm prediction shap-based model interpretation and two-way fixed effects twfe regression analysis
using provincial data for china 2003 2022 we first construct a composite epi with the entropy weight method then apply a sparrow search algorithm ssa to optimize lstm hyperparameters for epi forecasting
shap is used to interpret feature contributions to model-predicted epi and twfe regression is used to provide complementary panel-data evidence on factor-epi associations
the results show that the ssa-lstm model outperforms benchmark machine learning and deep learning models in out-of-sample prediction performance
shap-based interpretation indicates that variables such as gdp energy intensity and power generation per capita contribute strongly to prediction variation with notable regional heterogeneity
twfe results are broadly consistent with several key patterns identified in the shap analysis
overall the proposed framework provides an accurate and interpretable provincial energy poverty prediction approach and offers a useful empirical reference for energy poverty monitoring and policy discussion
introduction in the context of global energy transition and sustainable development 1 energy poverty has emerged as a critical issue that requires urgent attention worldwide 2
this is especially true in china where despite sustained economic growth and the widening urban-rural gap energy poverty remains a pressing concern in certain regions 3
energy poverty not only limits individuals access to basic living and production conditions but also undermines the economic development and environmental sustainability of society as a whole 4
the chinese government has made energy poverty alleviation a key policy objective implementing a series of measures such as rural grid renovation 5 and energy accessibility initiatives 6 achieving significant progress
however despite ongoing policy advancements energy poverty continues to be particularly pronounced in certain regions especially in the western and remote areas 7
this highlights the need for continuous optimization of energy poverty alleviation strategies and more precise evaluation and forecasting
therefore conducting research on energy poverty particularly through data-driven and accurate forecasting not only provides scientific evidence for policy formulation but also supports more equitable energy distribution and the coordinated development of socio-economic systems 8
against this background an important problem is how to construct a reliable provincial energy poverty index and develop an accurate interpretable and robust forecasting framework to identify the key factors associated with its spatiotemporal variation across regions in china
significant advancements have been made in energy poverty research with scholars employing a variety of methods to assess characterize and predict its extent across different regions and populations 9 10
traditional evaluation methods primarily rely on techniques such as the entropy weight method 11 and the critic method 12 which quantify the level of energy poverty by considering multiple dimensions including energy consumption 13 energy supply 14 and energy investment 15
common evaluation indicators include per capita electricity consumption 14 natural gas supply rate 16 and the proportion of low-carbon energy in the energy mix 17
these indicators provide a relatively clear reflection of the accessibility and cleanliness of energy services 18 19
such multidimensional index systems have laid an important foundation for measuring energy poverty and comparing regional disparities especially in contexts where direct household-level deprivation data are limited 20 21
however these conventional methods also have limitations when addressing complex regional heterogeneity dynamic temporal changes and interactions among multi-dimensional socioeconomic and environmental factors 22
in particular static or semi-static composite evaluation approaches may be less capable of capturing nonlinear relationships and evolving patterns in energy poverty across regions and over time 23
to overcome these challenges an increasing number of studies in recent years have adopted data-driven forecasting methods such as machine learning and deep learning models 24 to more accurately capture the trends and associated factors of energy poverty particularly in identifying the relative importance of variables in prediction tasks 25
compared with traditional evaluation approaches these methods have demonstrated considerable potential in improving prediction accuracy enhancing model adaptability and handling large-scale and high-dimensional datasets 26
at the same time the growing use of explainable machine learning techniques has further strengthened this research direction by enabling post hoc interpretation of model outputs and feature contributions 27 28
this helps improve the transparency of prediction models and provides additional analytical support for understanding why certain variables are more influential in model predictions 29 30
nevertheless existing studies still face challenges in balancing predictive performance and interpretability ensuring temporal robustness in model evaluation and clearly distinguishing model-based feature contribution from statistical association analysis 31 32
these issues suggest the need for a more integrated framework that combines robust prediction interpretable model explanation and complementary empirical analysis when examining energy poverty in a dynamic regional context 33
the prediction methods for energy poverty can generally be categorized into three main approaches
first mathematical statistical models such as multiple linear regression 34 and dynamic panel regression 35 are widely used in energy poverty analysis
these models predict energy poverty by establishing linear relationships between economic and social factors with the advantage of simplicity strong interpretability and ease of understanding and application
however these traditional statistical models often struggle to capture complex nonlinear relationships and high-dimensional interaction effects and they impose relatively stringent assumptions on the data which may result in insufficient prediction accuracy 36
secondly machine learning methods such as support vector machines 37 random forests 38 and xgboost 39 have gradually become mainstream in energy poverty prediction
these methods are effective in handling nonlinear relationships and high-dimensional data and they do not rely on rigid assumptions providing higher predictive accuracy 37 38 39
however machine learning models often lack sufficient interpretability presenting a challenge for policymakers in terms of how to interpret the predictions generated by these models 39
lastly hybrid optimization models such as optimized deep learning models 40 and ensemble learning methods 41 combine multiple models to leverage the strengths of each thereby enhancing prediction stability and accuracy
these methods are capable of addressing more complex scenarios and possess greater generalization ability though they may lead to increased computational costs and pose difficulties in model tuning due to the complexity of the combined models 40 41

data source and methods 2.1
data sources and variables selection the data used in this study were collected from the national bureau of statistics of china the national energy administration and annual provincial energy reports
the sample spans 2003 2022 and covers provincial indicators related to energy consumption energy supply energy investment and socioeconomic and environmental conditions
due to substantial data gaps and incomplete energy statistics tibet hong kong macau and taiwan were excluded from the analysis
before model construction the raw data were systematically preprocessed
specifically we harmonized statistical calibers and units across sources converted all variables into consistent numeric formats and checked variable names and annual records for consistency during panel-data merging
we also screened for obvious data entry inconsistencies and corrected formatting issues e g non-numeric symbols and inconsistent brackets or labels
after these cleaning and consistency checks the final province-year panel used in the empirical analysis contains complete observations for the target variable and all explanatory variables
the resulting dataset combines temporal and cross-sectional information to support the measurement and prediction of energy poverty across chinese provinces 13
to evaluate energy poverty this study constructs a multidimensional indicator system based on prior research 14
the indicator system includes three dimensions energy service accessibility esa energy consumption cleanliness ecc and household energy access hea 15
importantly the unit of analysis in this paper is the province-year rather than individual households or only rural households
therefore the index developed in this study is designed to measure a provincial composite energy poverty index epi rather than a strictly rural energy poverty index
at the provincial level energy poverty reflects the overall conditions of energy service provision and household energy use across different settlement contexts
for this reason the indicator system integrates both urban and rural dimensions
specifically esa captures the accessibility and adequacy of basic energy consumption supply and investment ecc reflects the cleanliness of energy use and energy structure and hea captures the availability of household energy-related facilities in both urban and rural settings
this integrated design is consistent with the objective of this study which is to compare interprovincial and intertemporal differences in energy poverty conditions and to forecast provincial epi dynamics using panel data
energy poverty refers to a condition in which individuals or groups cannot adequately access essential energy services required for basic living and production needs 17
in this study the epi is constructed from the indicators listed in table 2
the direction column in table 2 indicates the direction of contribution in index construction i e whether a higher indicator value is associated with a lower or higher epi score after standardization
for example per capita electricity consumption and per capita natural gas consumption are treated as negative-direction indicators because in general higher access to these services is associated with lower energy poverty 16
by contrast per capita so 2 emissions from residential activities are treated as a positive-direction indicator because higher emissions indicate less clean household energy use and correspondingly more severe energy poverty

calculating energy poverty index using the entropy weight method in this study we employ the entropy weight method ewm to calculate the energy poverty index epi at the provincial level in china
the entropy weight method is an objective weighting technique based on information entropy which assigns different weights to indicators based on their variability information content
this method is highly objective as it does not rely on expert judgment and can effectively avoid the biases associated with subjective weighting 45
initially the raw data must undergo standardization to eliminate the impact of differing units of measurement 45
the standardized data can be calculated using the following formula 1 xij xij-min xj max xj min xj where xij represents the raw data of the j-th indicator for the i-th province and xij is the standardized data min xj and max xj represent the minimum and maximum values of the j-th indicator across all provinces respectively
next we calculate the weight of each indicator using the following formula 45 2 pij xij i 1 mxij where pij represents the weight of the j-th indicator for the i-th province and m is the total number of samples i e the number of provinces 30
based on these weights we compute the information entropy for each indicator which measures the variability of the indicator 45
the entropy is calculated as follows 3 ej 1 ln m i 1 mpij ln pij where ej represents the entropy value for the j-th indicator
a higher entropy value indicates less information meaning the indicator contributes less to the total energy poverty index
finally the weight for each indicator is computed from the information entropy using the following formula 45 4 wj 1 ej j 1 n 1 ej where wj represents the weight of the j-th indicator and n is the total number of indicators
smaller entropy values correspond to larger weights indicating that the indicator contributes more to the overall energy poverty index
once the weights for each indicator are obtained the energy poverty index epi can be calculated by a weighted sum of the standardized indicators 5 epi j 1 nwjxj where xj is the standardized value of the j-th indicator and wj is its corresponding weight
the resulting epi reflects the overall level of energy poverty across the provinces
ssa-lstm model construction 2.3 1
sparrow search algorithm this study utilizes the sparrow search algorithm ssa to optimize the model for predicting energy poverty at the provincial level in china
ssa is a swarm intelligence optimization algorithm that simulates the foraging behavior of sparrows inspired by the natural phenomenon in which sparrow groups cooperate and compete to find the optimal solution during food search
the advantage of ssa lies in its ability to thoroughly explore the solution space and improve search efficiency by combining local and global information
compared to traditional optimization algorithms ssa exhibits strong global search capability especially in solving complex high-dimensional optimization problems 46
the core idea of ssa is to solve optimization problems by simulating the foraging behavior of sparrow groups
in ssa sparrow individuals are divided into two categories leader sparrows and follower sparrows
leader sparrows are responsible for guiding the entire group through the search space while follower sparrows rely on the position of the leader sparrow to conduct local searches
ssa iteratively updates the positions of both leader and follower sparrows to find the optimal solution to the optimization problem 47
in ssa the leader sparrow guides the group forward and its position update rule is as follows 46 6 xleader xleader xbest-xleader where xleader represents the position of the leader sparrow is a constant controlling the step size and xbest is the current best solution in the group
for follower sparrows their position updates depend on the state of the leader sparrow as well as other sparrows in the group
the update rule for follower sparrows is as follows 47 7 xfollower xfollower xleader-xfollower xrandom-xfollower where xfollower represents the position of the follower sparrow and are constants controlling the influence of the leader sparrow and the random jump respectively and xrandom represents the position of a randomly selected sparrow
ssa continuously adjusts the positions of the leader and follower sparrows to conduct both global and local searches
in each iteration ssa evaluates the fitness of the current position which corresponds to the objective function value and adjusts the search strategy based on changes in fitness
the algorithm terminates when the stopping criterion is met which in this case is the maximum number of iterations and the optimal solution is output
the algorithm framework is illustrated in figure 3
in terms of computational complexity let p denote the population size t the maximum number of iterations d the dimension of the search space i e the number of hyperparameters to be optimized and cf the computational cost of one fitness evaluation
in each iteration ssa mainly involves position updates for leader and follower sparrows population-level fitness evaluation and ranking selection operations
therefore the per-iteration complexity can be expressed as o t pd plogp pcf and the overall time complexity of ssa is approximately 47 8 o t pd plogp pcf
in the present study the fitness function is defined as the validation rmse of the lstm model so the dominant computational cost comes from repeated model training evaluation i e cf
as a result the practical computational burden of ssa-lstm is primarily determined by the population size the number of iterations and the cost of lstm training under each candidate parameter set
to balance optimization quality and computational efficiency we further compare different search algorithms and conduct a population-size sensitivity analysis in section 4.4
long short-term memory lstm neural network model this study utilizes long short-term memory lstm networks to model and predict the time series of energy poverty index ep at the provincial level in china
compared to traditional recurrent neural networks lstm effectively mitigates the vanishing gradient problem through its gating structure enabling it to capture the dynamic evolution of energy poverty across different time scales
the lstm layer updates the current state ht and cell state ct through three gate mechanisms the forget gate input gate and output gate
the forget gate is responsible for filtering historical memories
its core function is to adaptively decide which information from the cell state c should be retained weakened or discarded based on the current input xt and the previous hidden state ht-1
this helps avoid the accumulation of redundant or outdated information during time propagation improving the model's robustness to long-term dependencies and structural changes 48
its calculation is given by 9 ft wf ht-1 xt bf where ft represents the activation vector of the forget gate determining which information from the previous cell state needs to be forgotten ht is the current hidden state and w and b are weight matrices and bias terms used in the calculations for the forget gate input gate candidate cell state and output gate respectively
the sigmoid function compresses values between 0 and 1 indicating whether the model should forget this information closer to 0 means more likely to forget
the input gate controls the extent to which new information is written into the cell state deciding which parts of the current input features should be encoded and injected into the cell state
specifically the input gate uses the activation vector it to weight the candidate cell state c t performing selective memory of the new information
this allows the lstm to suppress the writing of ineffective information when facing noise disturbances or short-term fluctuations while quickly learning key features when structural changes occur 49
its calculation is given by 10 it wi ht-1 xt bic t tanhwc ht-1 xt bc where it is the activation vector of the input gate deciding which new information will be added to the cell state and c t is the candidate cell state containing potential new information to be added to the current cell state
the hyperbolic tangent function tanh modulates the nonlinearity of the information with outputs between 1 and 1
in the model the cell state is updated by merging the historical memory retained by the forget gate with the new information introduced by the input gate
this results in an accumulated representation of the long-term dependency characteristics of the energy poverty series providing a stable memory carrier for subsequent state propagation and prediction 48
its calculation is 11 ct ft ct-1 it c t where ct is the current cell state representing an accumulated representation of past information updated by considering both forgotten and new information and denotes element-wise multiplication
finally the output gate extracts the most contributing information from the updated cell state ct to generate the hidden state ht
the output gate uses the activation vector ot to determine which information from the cell state participates in subsequent calculations enabling effective expression of the current prediction task while retaining long-term memory 50
its calculation is 12 ot wo ht-1 xt boht ot tanhct where ot is the activation vector of the output gate determining which information from the cell state will be used to compute the current hidden state ht

influencing factor analysis and model interpretation 2.4 1
influencing factor analysis and model interpretation in this study shap shapley additive explanations values are used to interpret the ssa-lstm prediction model and identify the relative contribution of input features to model-predicted energy poverty levels at the provincial level in china
shap is derived from the shapley value in cooperative game theory and its core idea is to allocate the contribution of each feature to the model prediction by averaging its marginal contribution across all possible feature combinations 50
this provides a unified framework for interpreting feature importance and direction in nonlinear prediction models
the calculation formula for shap values is as follows 13 shap f x s n j s n-s-1 n fs j-fs where f s represents the prediction value of the feature set s s j is the feature set s augmented by feature j s is the size of the set s and n is the total number of features
shap values calculate each feature's contribution to the final prediction by considering the contributions of all feature combinations
based on the size and sign of the shap values we can identify which features have a significant positive or negative influence on the prediction of energy poverty
through shap value analysis we gain an in-depth understanding of the contribution of various features to the energy poverty index such as per capita regional gdp energy consumption intensity and forest coverage among others
this method not only quantifies the contribution of each feature but also effectively explains the model's prediction results enhancing the transparency and interpretability of the model
the framework for this method is shown in figure 5
two-way fixed effects regression for associated-factor analysis to complement the shap-based model interpretation and provide panel-data statistical evidence on factor-epi relationships this study further employs a two-way fixed effects twfe regression model
the twfe model controls for unobserved time-invariant provincial heterogeneity and common year shocks thereby allowing a more rigorous examination of the associations between key explanatory variables and the provincial energy poverty index
the baseline specification is as follows 37 14 epiit xit i t it where epiit denotes the provincial energy poverty index for province i in year t xit is a vector of explanatory variables e g economic demographic energy and environmental factors i represents province fixed effects t represents year fixed effects and it is the error term
in the twfe analysis the baseline specification uses lagged explanatory variables xi t-3 to align the timing structure with the prediction framework we additionally report results for xi t-1 and xi t-2 as robustness checks
the two-way fixed effects regression is used to assess whether the directional associations suggested by the shap-based model interpretation are statistically consistent in the panel-data setting
however we note that the twfe results should be interpreted as associational evidence rather than definitive causal effects unless additional identification strategies are introduced
in this study the twfe analysis is intended to provide complementary statistical support for interpreting the factors related to provincial energy poverty variation

index calculation model because most variables deviate from normality it is necessary to account for distributional properties before applying weighting and measurement procedures
accordingly this study proposes a critic-spearman approach in which spearman's rank correlation coefficient is used to quantify inter-indicator correlations thereby mitigating the influence of non-normality on correlation estimation
when the critic method is used alone it can assign weights based on the contrast intensity of indicators and their correlations but it may overlook informational uncertainty
in contrast the entropy weight method ewm incorporates information entropy to capture the uncertainty or dispersion of indicators however it does not adequately account for correlations among indicators
therefore we integrate critic-spearman with ewm to more comprehensively capture the joint effects of multiple dimensions of the digital economy
critic-spearman model the critic model is a weighting method that assigns indicator weights based on each indicator's standard deviation and its correlation with other indicators
in this study because most variables deviate from normality the conventional pearson correlation coefficient may be inappropriate

spearman's correlation is more robust and can effectively accommodate nonlinear associations and non-normally distributed data 33

to reduce the risk of overfitting and evaluate model generalizability this study further introduces an out-of-sample validation procedure using five-fold blocked cross-validation blocked k-fold
unlike standard k-fold cross-validation the temporal order of the data is preserved i e no random shuffling is applied
the time-ordered dataset is divided into five consecutive folds and in each iteration one fold is used as the validation set while the remaining four folds are used as the training set
this process is repeated until each fold has been used once as the validation set and the final validation performance is summarized using the average rmse mape and r 2 across the five folds
compared with a single holdout split blocked k-fold validation makes more efficient use of the available data and provides a more stable performance estimate
however because some folds may use observations both earlier and later than the validation block for training this approach does not represent a strictly forward-looking forecasting protocol
therefore in this study blocked k-fold cross-validation is used as a robustness check of model generalization to the overall data structure rather than a strict real-time forecasting test
in this study the in-sample evaluation metrics are rmse 122.008 mape 2.64 and r 2 0.9975
to assess out-of-sample performance the five-fold blocked cross-validation results are also reported yielding rmse 168.35 mape 5.56 and r 2 0.9236
the lower performance in cross-validation relative to the in-sample fit is expected and indicates that the model's predictive capability is weaker under out-of-sample evaluation
nevertheless the cross-validation results suggest that the model still captures the main trend of electricity production reasonably well
